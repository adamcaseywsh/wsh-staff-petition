Full text of the staff petition

TO: Governor of Virginia; Attorney General of Virginia; State Inspector General; and Virginia General Assembly committees responsible for behavioral health and government oversight.

We, the undersigned employees and supporters, request a fair, independent review of patient safety and staff accountability at Western State Hospital. Employees should be able to report suspected abuse, retaliation, neglect, or failures to report without fear of punishment. This petition does not presume wrongdoing by any employee.

Requested action

  • An independent investigation and periodic audit of complaints, abuse reports, retaliation allegations, and reporting practices.
  • Legally authorized employee integrity screening, including polygraph examinations when appropriate and lawful, with due process and employment-law safeguards.
  • Review of three subjects: (1) patient abuse or mistreatment; (2) retaliation for complaints; and (3) witnessing, concealing, or failing to report serious misconduct.
  • Confidential reporting outside hospital management, protection against retaliation, timely written complaint responses, and referrals when evidence supports disciplinary or criminal action.
  • A written response explaining what action the Commonwealth will take and whether legislation or policy changes are needed.

Reason for this request

Virginia Department of Corrections Operating Procedure 030.4 provides for authorized polygraph examinations in certain investigations. We request comparable attention to patient safety at state psychiatric hospitals—not an assertion that every corrections employee is already required to undergo testing. Patient rights are also protected by Virginia Code § 37.2-400 and 12VAC35-115. A polygraph alone is not proof of misconduct; independent evidence and fair procedures remain essential.

No patient or employee should be pressured to sign or punished for signing or declining to sign. Please protect the confidentiality of signers and avoid disclosure of patient treatment information.

Expanded requests for independent review

In addition to the original petition, staff supporters request that authorized independent reviewers consider the following measures. These are proposals for investigation and policy review, not findings of misconduct or statements of existing legal requirements.

  1. Independent intake: provide a reporting pathway outside the immediate supervisory chain, with clear instructions on confidentiality and jurisdiction.
  2. Evidence protection: promptly preserve relevant reports, incident logs, surveillance recordings, complaint histories, and related records in accordance with applicable law and retention obligations.
  3. Fair review: independently examine allegations, interview willing witnesses, distinguish substantiated findings from unverified claims, and afford affected employees fair process.
  4. Anti-retaliation: explain available protections and routes to report suspected reprisal; avoid promises of absolute confidentiality.
  5. Patient safeguards: review patient-rights complaint processes, timely responses, and whether vulnerable patients can access independent advocacy.
  6. Policy and legislative review: assess whether additional auditing, reporting, training, or lawful investigative screening authority is warranted; do not impose compulsory polygraphs without proper legal authority.
  7. Public accountability: request an agency response describing steps taken and any public findings that may lawfully be disclosed without exposing protected records.

How the staff initiative operates

  1. Read: review the full petition and proposed reforms before deciding whether to participate.
  2. Choose: staff members may independently endorse the request, decline, or ask questions. No signature is required to read the site.
  3. Send: select appropriate offices, copy the editable declaration, personalize it accurately, and send it from your own email account. Do not claim to represent colleagues without their permission.
  4. Report separately: use official confidential intake channels for firsthand abuse, neglect, retaliation, or safety allegations. Do not put patient details or confidential evidence in mass emails.
  5. Follow up: request acknowledgments and substantive responses; document only nonconfidential public milestones in the accountability log.

Important: email recipients may see your identity and workplace email. Neither the site nor email can guarantee anonymity or legal whistleblower protection. Staff should consider using a personal device and account outside work when permitted, and consult a qualified advisor about applicable employment rules.

Independent review and safeguards

Before collecting or submitting signed staff sheets, seek independent legal or employee-representative review of privacy, retaliation, employment-law, and polygraph language. A polygraph is not conclusive evidence, and any testing must be legally authorized and fairly administered. Do not publish completed signature sheets or staff allegations. This informational site does not replace emergency reporting, mandatory reporting duties, or legal advice.

Report misconduct, cooperate, and seek protection

For current or former staff with firsthand information: You can request independent review of suspected patient abuse, neglect, retaliation, concealment, falsified records, or failure to report. Describe what you personally observed, when it occurred, who may have records, and whether anyone is currently at risk. Distinguish facts from suspicions.

Reporting is not automatic immunity or a release from fault. Good-faith reporting and cooperation may support legal protections or mitigation in appropriate circumstances, but they do not automatically erase misconduct, prevent discipline, excuse mandatory reporting duties, or guarantee immunity. If you believe you may have personal legal exposure, consult an independent attorney or union/employee representative before giving a detailed statement.

How to make a responsible report

  1. Protect safety first. For immediate danger use emergency channels. Follow any applicable mandatory reporting requirements without waiting for this petition.
  2. Choose a suitable independent channel. OSIG complaint intake or dLCV service request; ask the agency whether it has jurisdiction and what confidentiality protections apply.
  3. Preserve lawfully. Identify dates, incident numbers, record locations and potential witnesses; do not take, copy, alter, destroy or publish patient records or restricted employment records without authorization.
  4. Request protection. Explain any threatened retaliation and ask the receiving agency how to submit safely and how to follow up.
  5. Cooperate truthfully. Do not coordinate stories, make unsupported accusations, or promise colleagues immunity.

Privacy: Do not enter incident details into this website. It has no reporting form, does not receive complaints, and cannot guarantee anonymity. Sending email may expose your identity and metadata. Use the agency's secure intake process when available.
